    What this report cannot tell you

    The Matterhorn Protocol defines 136 failure conditions for PDF/UA-1. Only 87 can be determined by software, and this tool implements a subset of those. A clean report means nothing automatable is wrong with the file. Whether the reading order makes sense, whether headings describe their sections, and whether a description actually conveys what a figure shows are all still yours to check.

    How it works

    A screen reader does not read a page the way you see it. It follows a tag tree stored in the file, and most PDFs do not have one. The text is there, but it arrives in whatever order the producer happened to write it, with no indication of what is a heading, what is a caption, and what is decoration.

    This rebuilds that structure: it marks real content, tags images as figures with their bounding boxes, recovers the mapping from character codes to actual text out of the embedded font programs, and writes the PDF/UA identification into the document metadata. Then it audits its own output and reports what is left.

    What it will not do

    It will not write alternate text for you. A model can produce a plausible sentence about an image, and a plausible sentence about a chart of clinical outcomes is worse than no sentence at all, because a reader has no way to know it is wrong. Undescribed figures are reported instead.

    It will not embed a font that is not in the file, and it will not guess what a character code means when nothing in the document says. Codes that cannot be resolved are left unmapped and counted, rather than filled with spaces to make a checker pass.

    Uploaded documents and their results are deleted after one hour. Nothing is sent to a third party.
